# M1.6b.2 — Physics-Based Incidence Loss

## Scope

This milestone introduces a flow-dependent incidence-loss
closure into the nonlinear centrifugal compressor model.

The implementation includes:

- An independent incidence-loss kernel.
- Design-point inlet blade-angle calibration.
- Operating-point velocity-triangle evaluation.
- Entropy-generation coupling to the nonlinear solver.
- A paired compressor-map benchmark.
- An analytical zero-incidence locus.
- An independent algebraic loss identity.

The implementation is CPU-based. GPU execution remains future work.

## Incidence model

The inlet relative flow angle is

    beta_1 = atan2(V_m1, U_1 - V_theta1)

with

    V_m1 = mass_flow / (rho_1 * A_1)

and

    U_1 = pi * D_1 * N / 60.

The incidence angle is

    i = beta_1 - beta_b1.

The specific incidence loss is

    L_inc = 0.5 * K_inc * W_1^2 * sin(i)^2.

An equivalent algebraic form is

    L_inc = 0.5 * K_inc *
        [V_m1*cos(beta_b1)
         - (U_1 - V_theta1)*sin(beta_b1)]^2.

These expressions are mathematically equivalent within
the implemented inlet velocity-triangle model.

## Nonlinear coupling

Incidence loss is converted into an approximate
entropy-generation contribution:

    delta_s_inc = L_inc / T_t1.

The prescribed baseline entropy generation is augmented:

    delta_s_total = delta_s_baseline + delta_s_inc.

The existing nonlinear solver then determines the
outlet thermodynamic state.

This closure is an engineering approximation.
It is not a detailed irreversible-flow calculation.

## Calibration

The inlet blade angle is calibrated for zero incidence at:

    mass flow = 5 kg/s
    speed = 12000 rpm
    inlet temperature = 300 K
    inlet pressure = 500000 Pa
    inlet prewhirl = 20 m/s.

The incidence coefficient is K_inc = 1.

This coefficient is exploratory and has not been
calibrated against experimental compressor data.

The current three-EOS comparison calibrates a separate
blade angle for each thermodynamic model.

Consequently, the three cases do not represent an
identical manufactured impeller operating with
three different gases.

A fixed-geometry cross-gas comparison is future work.

## Analytical zero-incidence locus

For the supported inlet velocity-triangle regime,

    mass_flow_zero =
        rho_1 * A_1 *
        (U_1 - V_theta1) * tan(beta_b1).

Under fixed inlet density and prewhirl, eliminating
the calibrated blade angle gives

    mass_flow_zero(N) =
        mass_flow_design *
        [U_1(N) - V_theta1] /
        [U_1(N_design) - V_theta1].

The verified zero-incidence mass flows are:

| Speed (rpm) | Mass flow (kg/s) |
|---:|---:|
| 9000 | 3.41329 |
| 10500 | 4.20664 |
| 12000 | 5.00000 |
| 13500 | 5.79336 |
| 15000 | 6.58671 |

All three independently calibrated models reproduce
this locus under the specified inlet conditions.

## Operating-envelope validation

The benchmark covers:

- Seven mass flows: 2 through 8 kg/s.
- Five speeds: 9000 through 15000 rpm.
- Three thermodynamic models:
  - Air-like ideal gas.
  - Methane ideal gas.
  - Methane Peng-Robinson.

The benchmark performs:

- 105 baseline nonlinear solves.
- 105 zero-incidence-coefficient regression solves.
- 105 incidence-aware nonlinear solves.

All 315 solutions passed the benchmark acceptance checks.

The resulting 105 paired operating points passed
independent CSV validation.

## Numerical results

| Quantity | Air-like ideal | Methane ideal | Methane PR |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Maximum incidence loss (J/kg) | 490.948 | 1082.573 | 1070.057 |
| Mean incidence entropy (J/kg/K) | 0.407552 | 0.897667 | 0.887280 |
| Mean static pressure-ratio change | -0.001901 | -0.002098 | -0.002099 |
| Mean total pressure-ratio change | -0.002459 | -0.002379 | -0.002385 |
| Mean outlet-temperature change (K) | -0.003225 | -0.005825 | -0.010522 |
| Mean specific-work change (J/kg) | -8.084 | -19.296 | -19.078 |
| Mean Euler-power change (W) | -49.744 | -118.471 | -117.130 |

All reported pressure-ratio changes are
incidence-aware minus baseline.

Across the tested envelope, the incidence-aware
total pressure ratio never exceeds the baseline value.

The computed Euler work and Euler power also decrease
slightly because the outlet density and exit velocity
triangle respond to the modified entropy closure.

These results do not establish a complete mechanical
shaft-power model.

## Limitations

1. The incidence coefficient is not experimentally calibrated.
2. Inlet blade angle is represented by a single meanline value.
3. Spanwise incidence and blade loading are not resolved.
4. Diffusion, separation, and detailed viscous losses are
   not independently modeled.
5. The entropy conversion uses the inlet stagnation
   temperature as an approximation.
6. Slip factor remains prescribed.
7. The calculated Euler power is not a complete mechanical
   shaft-power requirement.
8. Surge and choke boundaries remain unevaluated.
9. GPU acceleration has not yet been implemented.
10. Compressor efficiency optima are not established by
    the zero-incidence locus alone.

## Next direction
